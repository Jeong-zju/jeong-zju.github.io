From capable robots to capable teams

COLLABORATIVE ROBOT LEARNING

The problem

Every new agent changes the problem

A team can be full of capable robots and still fail together. My work asks how embodied agents can read one another through motion, contact, and shared objects, then adapt as each action reshapes the possibilities available to the rest of the team.

DECENTRALIZED · EMBODIED · CONTINGENT

Research thesis

The interaction is the unit of intelligence.

LOCAL VIEWS / TEAM STATE

One 3B model runs independently on every robot at 30 Hz, using local observations and history rather than a central policy or privileged team state. Adding an agent does not require a larger centralized action space.

CONTINUITY / LONG-HORIZON EXECUTION

In evaluation, one policy runs for more than ten minutes across eight collaborative subtasks—without task resets or policy switching.

CONTACT / PARTNER RESPONSE

Across held-out collaborative decisions, predictive introspection selects the action leading to better partner behavior in 87% of cases, versus 61% without lookahead.

I build policies that can read the interaction, carry its history forward, and choose actions that leave the team with better options.

When a team shares objects and workspaces, timing becomes part of dexterity. I train decentralized whole-body policies to wait, yield, hold, regrasp, and resume as teammates and contacts evolve.

About

I am Zihao Li, a PhD researcher building physical intelligence for robots that share the world.

At the University of Sydney, I work across multi-robot policy learning, long-horizon memory, predictive action selection, and force-aware whole-body manipulation. Previously, I worked with the Computational Robotics Lab at ETH Zürich and studied at Zhejiang University. Across these problems, I return to one question: how do individually capable robots become capable teammates?
