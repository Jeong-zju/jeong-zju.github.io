Overview

Bringing shared belief into the physical world

Robots rarely see the same world at the same time. I build learning systems that let embodied agents preserve uncertain evidence, exchange useful beliefs, and coordinate action across long-horizon tasks.

My Thesis

Robot intelligence should be collective.

A useful belief captures what an agent knows, what it doubts, and what another robot needs.

Coordination emerges when local observations become shared intent.

Learning must remain grounded when evidence arrives late, partially, or not at all.

I develop the representations, policies, and physical systems needed to turn this thesis into real robot behavior.

About

I am Zihao Li, a PhD researcher in Computer Science at the University of Sydney.

My work spans robot manipulation, embodied intelligence, vision-language-action models, and multi-agent learning. Previously, I worked with the Computational Robotics Lab at ETH Zürich and studied at Zhejiang University.
